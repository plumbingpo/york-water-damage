AC Leak Water Damage Help in York

Your air conditioner pulls moisture out of the air all day, and that water has to go somewhere. Normally it drips off the coil into a pan and leaves through a small drain line. When that line clogs with algae and dust, the pan fills and spills over. If the air handler sits in a hallway closet or the attic, that water ends up in the nearby floor, wall or ceiling.

Slow leaks do more damage than people expect. Drywall draws the water in, the platform beneath the unit gets saturated, and moisture slides under the floor, with mold often underway before any stain appears. We find how far the moisture spread, dry the structure and repair what was damaged. An HVAC technician still has to fix the unit or unclog the line, and we plan our work around that appointment.

Do This First

Your First Steps

  1. Shut the System DownSwitch the system off at the thermostat so it stops making condensate. If water is near the air handler's wiring, turn off its breaker as well.
  2. Keep Clear of Wet ElectricalCeiling lights, outlets and the unit itself are off limits while wet, and stay that way until their circuit has been turned off.
  3. Clear Water From the PanUse a wet-vac or rags to take the standing water out of the pan and stop the spill, provided the pan is safe to reach. Crawling over a wet attic to get there is not worth it.
  4. Dry the Easy SpotsPress towels into damp carpet, mop hard flooring, put a bucket below any drip from the ceiling, and clear furniture, clothes and boxes out of the closet or away from the stain.
  5. Bring In Two ProsAn HVAC technician should clear the drain line, and our team should find out where the water went. Keep the AC off until the repair is made.
What We Do

Our AC Leak Water Damage Process

1

Measure the Spread

With thermal imaging and moisture meters, our team checks the unit, the adjacent walls or ceiling and the floor beyond the closet, so wet materials are found even where no stain shows.

2

Remove Water and Wet Drywall

We extract the water sitting in the carpet, the platform and the pan. To let the space behind it breathe, we cut away drywall and insulation that are soaked or growing mold.

3

Dry the Structure

Dehumidifiers and air movers stay on for a few days, usually 3 to 5, and we log the moisture readings. We place equipment so the HVAC technician can still reach the unit.

4

Clean and Restore

Days-old condensate is not clean water, so everything it reached is treated with an antimicrobial. After that, our crew puts back the drywall, insulation, baseboard and floor and paints the repair to blend in.

Will Insurance Pay?

What About Insurance?

Timing is usually what decides whether an AC leak is covered. If the pan overflowed suddenly and you acted when you found it, many homeowners policies pay for the resulting water damage. A drip the adjuster believes lasted weeks or months can be classed as gradual leakage or poor maintenance, and the claim denied. Either way, the cost of fixing the AC unit is typically left out. Check your policy, and we can supply readings and photos for the claim.

Why It Happens

Why This Happens

  • Units that rely on a condensate pump, when that pump quits or gets unplugged.
  • More meltwater than the pan can handle after an iced-over coil thaws.
  • A dirty filter or low refrigerant, both of which make the coil ice up.
  • A drain line joint that came apart or was never glued, leaking inside a wall or attic.
  • Missing insulation on cold lines and ductwork, so condensation forms and falls.
Why Speed Matters

What Happens if You Wait

Ceilings Under the Unit

Overflow from a unit in the attic lands on the ceiling drywall. First comes a stain, next a sag, and after days of soaking a piece may drop into the bedroom or hall underneath.

Mold Around the Air Handler

Mold may appear in 24 to 48 hours on damp materials by the air handler, where the return air stream can carry its spores through the ductwork to other rooms.

Baseboards and Door Trim

Baseboards and door casings near the closet draw water up from the floor. Swollen fiberboard pieces do not recover and are normally swapped for new ones.

Floors Outside the Closet

From the closet, water slips under the door and works below hardwood, laminate or carpet pad. Laminate swells and will not recover, and hardwood cups if it stays wet.
