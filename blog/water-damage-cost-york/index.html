How Much Does Water Damage Cost in York?

Home / Blog / How Much Does Water Damage Cost in York?

Typical Cost at a Glance

Typical York water damage projects cost from $1,500 up to $10,000 or more. With clean water and a small area, the total can stay below about $1,500. Major flooding or a sewage backup that needs rebuilding can run well above $10,000.

None of this is a quote, only the usual range across the country. Pricing in York, PA depends on local wages and the going rate for materials, so your number may differ. The only way to get an exact figure is an on-site inspection with moisture readings.

How Water Type Affects Cost

The first thing a restoration estimate looks at is what kind of water it is. Contamination level places every loss in one of three industry categories. As contamination rises, so does the need for cleaning, tear-out of materials and protective equipment.

Depending on which category applies, US cleanup costs are usually in the area of $3 to $7.50 a square foot. Here are the usual numbers.

CategoryWhat it isTypical cost per sq ft
Category 1, clean waterSupply lines, faucets, water heater tanksAbout $3 to $4
Category 2 (gray water)Failed sump pumps, discharge from a dishwasher or washing machineAbout $4 to $6.50
Category 3 (black water)Storm surge, flooding rivers, sewageAbout $7 to $7.50

The category is not fixed. After about two days of sitting, water that started clean carries bacteria and is reclassified to gray or black. Putting things off raises the price for this reason, among others.

What Affects the Cost Most

Two homes with the same broken pipe can get very different bills. These are the items a contractor weighs in an estimate.

  • Area affected. More wet square footage means more labor, more equipment and more days on site.
  • Water category. Handling is least expensive when the water is clean. Nothing costs more to deal with than sewage.
  • How long it sat. The longer water sits, the deeper it gets into walls and the subfloor. A leak found in an hour costs far less than one found after a weekend away.
  • Materials. Tile and concrete dry well. Special drying or outright replacement is common for drywall, hardwood, insulation and carpet pad.
  • Mold. If growth has started, mold remediation is added to the scope, with containment and air filtration.
  • Reconstruction. The drying phase has its own price. Putting back cabinets, paint, flooring and drywall is billed separately, and for a big loss that part is frequently larger.

Estimates usually cover a pair of phases, extraction of the water and then drying of the structure. Drying usually takes 3 to 5 days.

A daily rate is common for dehumidifiers and air movers. A home that dries in three days costs less than one that takes six.

Cost by Common Job

For the kinds of losses that come up most frequently in York, national figures usually look like this. Use the numbers to get oriented and not as a firm price.

  • Burst pipe. Often $1,500 to $5,000 for cleanup and drying, plus the burst pipe repair itself. A pipe that ran for hours, or wet more than one level, costs more.
  • Appliance leak. A washing machine, dishwasher or water heater leak caught early is often $1,000 to $4,000. When a slow drip goes unnoticed below cabinets, concealed damage raises the price.
  • Basement flood. The usual range is $2,000 to $10,000 or more, varying with the water's source, its depth and any finished surfaces. Finished basements land near the top of the range for basement water removal.
  • Sewage backup. Many jobs cost $3,000 to $10,000, some more. Anything porous has to go and all surfaces get disinfected, since cleaning up sewage counts as Category 3.
  • Roof leak. Without the roof repair itself, drying and fixing walls, insulation and ceilings is often $1,000 to $5,000.

Is Water Damage in York Covered by Insurance?

It depends on the cause and on your policy. A typical homeowners policy often covers accidental events that happen suddenly, for example a pipe bursting or a supply line giving out with no warning.

Gradual leaks are usually not covered. An insurer may treat a fitting that dripped for months as a maintenance problem. Unless you have a separate flood policy, water that enters from outside, like storm surge or a rising river, is usually excluded. A backed-up sewer often takes an added endorsement.

Check your policy or call your agent before you assume anything. Remember to consider your deductible. If the work is minor, its cost may be below your deductible and there may be little point in a claim.

Ways to Lower the Cost

How much you pay for water damage is largely settled within a day or two. Some basic actions go a long way.

  • Act fast. Until someone removes it, water continues to spread. Every hour adds wet material.
  • Shut off the water. The moment you discover a leak, close the main shutoff, which means knowing in advance where to find it.
  • Document everything. Shoot video and pictures first, while everything is still in place and nothing has been tossed. Keep receipts.
  • Call a pro before mold starts. Within 24 to 48 hours, mold growth can get underway. After that point, expect added time and added cost.
  • Move what you can. Get rugs, furniture and boxes off wet floors if it is safe.

If the power has not been cut, never wade into standing water, and avoid any water that could carry sewage. No saving is worth a shock or an infection. If the water came from outside, leave flood cleanup to a crew with the right protective gear.

Getting a Real Number

Averages from across the country are fine for planning and still cannot describe your home. An inspection with a moisture meter shows how far the water travelled and what it will take to dry and repair.

If you need water damage restoration in York, PA, the best time to ask for that inspection is now, while the damage is still small.
