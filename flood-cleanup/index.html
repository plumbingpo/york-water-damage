Floods start in many ways: heavy rain, storm surge, a river over its banks, a dead sump pump, a sewer backup or a major plumbing failure, and all of them call for fast professional help. In nearly every case the water falls into Category 2, known as gray water, or Category 3, known as black water, which means contaminants, bacteria and other hazards that are a real health risk. Day or night, 24/7, York Restoration sends flood cleanup crews throughout York to remove floodwater, sanitize the affected rooms and safely restore your home or building.

The first job is getting the water out quickly, and our submersible and truck-mounted pumps are able to clear thousands of gallons every hour. With the standing water gone, our IICRC-certified technicians check the contamination level and apply the right sanitizing steps, including antimicrobial treatment, HEPA air filtration and disinfection of every affected surface. Anything unsalvageable comes out and is hauled off correctly, including wet insulation, drywall that took in contaminated water and waterlogged carpet padding.

Then the drying begins: high-velocity air movers and commercial dehumidifiers of the LGR (low-grain refrigerant) type go in to pull moisture out of the building's structural parts. Daily readings from calibrated moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera tell us when everything is fully dry, which is what keeps mold from growing. Every stage of the job is recorded for your claim: photographs, detailed moisture maps, logs of the equipment used and the scope-of-work reports that insurance adjusters require.

Signs You Need Flood Cleanup in York

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Tide marks on drywall or trim where the water peaked
  • Soaked landscaping that leaves water pooling against your foundation
  • An overflowing washing machine, dishwasher or water heater
  • Water pooled on any floor of the building, no matter where it came from
  • Water coming in through foundation cracks, window wells or door thresholds
  • Heavy rainfall, a dead sump pump and water spreading across the basement
  • Receding water that leaves muddy residue and debris behind

Flood Cleanup Prevention Tips

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Cracks in the foundation should be sealed and the basement walls given a waterproof coating
  • Check that grading on all sides of the house carries water away from the foundation
  • Keep gutters and storm drains clear of debris so water can flow properly
  • In areas of the property that tend to flood, raise electrical systems and appliances off the floor
  • Look into a flood insurance policy, including when your home sits outside a mapped flood zone
  • Use waterproof bins and raised shelving for anything valuable kept at ground level or below

Why Flood Cleanup Is Not a DIY Job

Flood cleanup is far more than pumping out standing water. The water brings sewage, chemicals, bacteria and more with it, and all of that is a serious hazard to health. A trained cleanup team knows how to set containment, wear PPE (personal protective equipment) correctly and sanitize by protocol, which protects your household from hepatitis, E. coli and other diseases carried in water.

Health is not the only issue: when cleanup is done wrong, moisture stays hidden and heavy mold can follow in a matter of days. With thermal imaging, psychrometric calculations and moisture meters, trained technicians confirm that subfloors, structural framing, wall cavities and every other wet area dry to an acceptable standard. Drying by measurement is the one dependable method for avoiding secondary damage, which can add thousands to the repair bill.
