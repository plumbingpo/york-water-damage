A sewage backup is one of the most hazardous situations a property owner can face. Raw sewage contains dangerous bacteria, viruses, parasites and other pathogens, including E. coli, hepatitis, salmonella and giardia, that pose serious health risks to anyone who touches contaminated materials. When it happens in York, York Restoration is available 24/7 with technicians trained for this work, proper protective gear and disinfectants registered with the EPA, everything needed for a safe decontamination.

The industry classifies sewage as black water, or Category 3, which is the most dangerous and most contaminated type of water damage there is. This classification requires the most stringent cleanup protocols, including full personal protective equipment, containment barriers, removal of all porous materials that contacted sewage and thorough disinfection of every remaining surface. Our technicians follow IICRC S500 standards for Category 3 water damage so your property is restored to safe occupancy standards.

We clear the standing sewage first, then pull contaminated water out of structural materials, remove carpet, padding, insulation, drywall and any other porous item that was in contact with sewage for proper disposal, disinfect each hard surface, treat with antimicrobial products and finish with structural drying, which prevents secondary mold. Lingering sewage odor is eliminated with thermal fogging and industrial hydroxyl generators, which we run in the affected area.

Signs It Is Time for Sewage Cleanup

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • A rotten egg or sewer smell from the drains or the lowest floor of the home
  • A toilet overflow containing waste that has spread onto flooring or carpet
  • People in the household getting stomach or intestinal symptoms with no clear cause
  • Multiple drains in your home backing up at the same time
  • Toilets or drains that gurgle whenever you run water somewhere else
  • A cleanout that weeps sewage, or wet foul patches in the lawn along the sewer path
  • Foul-smelling moisture showing up on flooring or along the walls

Sewage Cleanup: What Homeowners Ask

Most backups start with a blockage in the home's sewer line. Roots from nearby trees, flushed wipes and accumulated grease account for a large share of those clogs. Older lines can also crack or collapse, and heavy rain can overload the municipal sewer and push sewage back through floor drains. A camera run through the line after cleanup identifies the actual cause.

Maintenance Tips for Sewage Cleanup

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Food waste, oils and grease block pipes, so put them in the trash and not the sink
  • Wipes clog sewer lines even when the label says flushable, so only toilet paper goes down the toilet
  • A drain that is slowing down should be dealt with early, while it is still partly open
  • Route sump pump and gutter water away from the sewer line, which can back up when storm flow overloads it
  • Find your sewer cleanout now and check its condition, so it can be reached in an emergency
  • Have your sewer line inspected with a camera every 2 to 3 years to catch potential problems

Why Professional Sewage Cleanup Matters

No homeowner should try to clean up sewage personally. Because raw sewage is a biological hazard, the work demands trained people, protective equipment (gloves, boots, respirators and Tyvek suits) and disinfection products registered with the EPA and not sold to the public. People exposed to the pathogens in sewage can become seriously ill with skin and respiratory infections, hepatitis or gastrointestinal disease.

Professional sewage cleanup also requires proper disposal of contaminated materials as biohazard waste. Simply placing sewage-contaminated drywall, carpet and insulation in your regular trash is both illegal and hazardous to sanitation workers and the community. Certified restoration companies maintain proper waste disposal protocols and relationships with licensed biohazard waste disposal facilities.
