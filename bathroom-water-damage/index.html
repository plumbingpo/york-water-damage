Bathroom Water Damage in York

Tile, tubs and fixtures are made to get wet on top, but what lies beneath them is not. Every flush and every shower lets a small amount of water by after the caulk cracks, the grout wears down or the seal at the toilet fails. That water gathers where you cannot see it, in the subfloor and inside the tiled wall. Our York team finds where the water is getting in, measures how far it has gone, and dries or removes what is wet.

Because the leak is usually slow, the damage tends to be older and deeper than it looks. The tile can look perfectly fine while the subfloor beneath the toilet has gone soft clear through. We use moisture meters and thermal imaging to map the wet area without tearing out more than we need to, then open only the sections that cannot be dried in place.

Warning Signs

  • The room keeps a damp, musty smell even right after a thorough cleaning
  • Dark grout that never seems to dry out between showers
  • A warped or swollen cabinet floor under the vanity sink
  • On the wall beside the shower, baseboard that has swelled or paint that is peeling
  • Floor that feels spongy by the vanity or next to the tub
  • Floor tiles that sound hollow, rock underfoot, or have come loose near the tub
The Clock

How the Damage Progresses

The first few days

Water Gets Past

A little water passes the failed grout or caulk every time someone showers. Backer board and the outer rim of the subfloor take it in, and there is still nothing to see.

First few weeks

Subfloor Stays Wet

Between showers, the wood below the tile has no time to dry out. Mold starts on the underside of the floor and inside the wall cavity.

One to three months

Signs Show Up

The caulk line goes black, the grout gets darker, and tiles work loose. A stain shows on the ceiling of any room below and slowly grows.

Six months and beyond

Structure Weakens

Joists can begin to rot, and the subfloor near the toilet or tub becomes soft. The repair now includes carpentry and new tile, not only drying.

How We Fix It

Our Bathroom Water Damage Process

1

Find the Leak

One by one we check the supply lines, toilet, shower and tub, then follow the water under the tile and into the walls with meters and a thermal camera.

2

Open What Is Needed

We take out rotted subfloor, soft wall board and tile that has come loose. A toilet leak means contamination, so anything porous that will not come clean is removed.

3

Dry the Cavities

The subfloor, the framing in the walls and the space above the lower ceiling are dried with air movers and dehumidifiers. We apply antimicrobial treatment and confirm dry readings before anything is closed up.

4

Rebuild and Seal

We replace subfloor and backer board, and can coordinate tile, a new toilet seal, fresh caulk and ceiling repair so the leak path is closed, not covered over.

What You Cannot See

What Hides Behind It

Mold Behind Tile

By themselves, grout and tile do not keep water out. Mold takes hold on the wall board once that hidden layer remains wet, and no scrubbing can get to it.

The Room Below

Before dripping, water runs along joists and pipes, which can put the downstairs stain several feet away from the real leak. Wiring and light fixtures may lie along that route.

Leaks Inside the Vanity

Behind the towels and bottles kept under the sink, a drain fitting or supply valve can drip for months. First the bottom of the cabinet swells, and then the wall behind and the subfloor get wet.

Rot Under the Toilet

When the wax ring gives out, a bit of water escapes every time the toilet is flushed. The water is category 3, and it rots the subfloor around the flange until the toilet starts to rock.
