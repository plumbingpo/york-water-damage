Kitchen Water Damage in York

Count the dishwasher, disposal, drain, faucet and fridge line, and the kitchen has more places for water to connect than anywhere else in the house. Nearly all of them are hidden inside a cabinet or behind an appliance. If one starts leaking, water heads for the back of the base cabinet and works its way under the floor. For homeowners in York, our job is to track it down, dry it out, and preserve all of the kitchen that can be saved.

Cabinets are the expensive part of this problem. Plywood and solid wood will often dry successfully, whereas a particleboard box puffs up quickly and never returns to size. By removing toe kicks and drilling small holes where they will not show, we push dry air beneath and behind the cabinets, and in many kitchens the countertops and cabinets never move.

Warning Signs

  • Downstairs, a brownish mark on the ceiling beneath the kitchen
  • Soft or discolored vinyl flooring near the refrigerator or along the cabinet line
  • Opening the sink cabinet releases a stale, damp odor
  • Sticking cabinet doors, or doors whose edges no longer match up
  • Water stains or bubbled paint on the wall behind the sink or dishwasher
  • Side panels on the base cabinets that are flaking or swollen at the bottom
What You Cannot See

What Hides Behind It

Wet Wall Behind Cabinets

Behind the dishwasher and sink, the wall carries both the drain and the supply lines. A leak there wets drywall and framing that the cabinets cover completely, so mold can spread across the wall unseen.

The Ice Maker Line

A few drops per minute can escape from the narrow tube that supplies the ice maker. Since the refrigerator is never pulled out, even that much rots the floor and the wall in back of it.

Behind the Dishwasher

Slowly dripping supply or drain fittings soak the cabinet sides beside the unit and the subfloor below. Until the floor changes, the dishwasher covers everything.

Drips at the Sink Drain

Because it leaks just when the sink drains, a loose fitting or a worn disposal seal often goes unnoticed. The water carries food waste, which is why a wet sink cabinet smells and molds so fast.

Why Speed Matters

The Damage Timeline

First hour

Water Goes Underneath

Water from a failed hose or fitting flows to the back of the cabinets and under the appliances. It slips beneath flooring through the seams and edges.

24 to 48 hours

Cabinets Start Swelling

Toe kicks and particleboard cabinet bases absorb the water and swell. Mold can begin in the dark, closed space under the cabinets and behind the dishwasher.

Day 3 to 7

Flooring Starts to Move

Hardwood cups, laminate edges puff up, and vinyl starts to lift as its adhesive lets go. Drawers and cabinet doors stop lining up.

Past one week

Tear Out Likely

Swollen cabinet boxes cannot be brought back. Subfloor may be delaminating, and mold behind the cabinets often means removing them along with the countertop above.

Our Process

How We Handle Kitchen Water Damage

1

Stop the Source

The fixture or appliance line that leaked is shut off, the refrigerator or dishwasher is pulled if necessary, and we confirm the failed connection so it gets repaired before we start drying.

2

Measure the Wet Area

The base of each cabinet, the wall in back and flooring all the way across the kitchen are checked with meters and a thermal camera. Under kitchen flooring, water often goes farther than anyone expects.

3

Dry in Place

We remove the toe kicks and direct air behind and beneath the cabinets. For several days the dehumidifiers and air movers keep running, and damp cavities receive an antimicrobial treatment.

4

Take Out Only What Failed

Cabinet sections, flooring or subfloor that cannot be saved are taken out carefully. We confirm dry readings, then reinstall appliances and help you line up any repairs that remain.
