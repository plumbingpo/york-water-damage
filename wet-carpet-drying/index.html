Wet Carpet Drying in York

There is always more water in a soaked carpet than the surface suggests. Often the pad is full of water and the subfloor is wet beneath it, even when the fibers you touch feel only slightly damp. Towels and a shop vacuum reach the top layer and little else. In York, we pull the water out, take moisture meter readings on each layer, and place drying equipment that works down to the subfloor.

Whether the carpet can be saved depends on two things, how clean the water was and how long it has been wet. If the water came out of a clean supply line and we reach it fast, the carpet can normally stay down while it dries. Gray water from an appliance often means the pad goes and the carpet gets cleaned and treated. After sewage or flood water from outdoors, neither the carpet nor the pad stays.

Signs to Look For

  • Lift a corner and the backing peels away from the carpet
  • Paint blistering near the floor, or baseboard trim that has puffed up
  • Soft or squeaky subfloor beneath the area of carpet that was soaked
  • Ripples or loose spots where the carpet has let go of the tack strip
  • Socks that come away damp, or a squishing sound, when you cross the carpet
  • A wet spot that keeps growing even though no new water has been added
Behind the Surface

The Damage You Do Not See

Clean Water Turns Dirty

Water that started clean does not stay that way. Soil and bacteria build up in it inside the carpet and pad, and a carpet that was dryable can end up being torn out.

Wet Wall Bottoms

Under the carpet, water travels until it hits the bottom of the walls. Because carpet covers the edge, no one sees the drywall and baseboards drawing the water upward.

Swollen Subfloor

Left wet, the OSB or particleboard beneath a carpet swells and then crumbles. Squeaks, soft spots and raised seams show up weeks after the spill is forgotten.

Water Held in the Pad

A carpet pad absorbs many times what it weighs in water. It keeps feeding moisture into the carpet above and the floor below long after the surface feels dry.

If Nothing Is Done

The Damage Timeline

The first few hours

Water Moves Outward

Under the surface, the pad pulls water outward, well beyond what looks wet. It moves under walls and into the next room, and the tack strip starts soaking it up.

24 to 48 hours

Smell and Mold Start

The damp pad breeds bacteria, and the sour smell of wet carpet follows. Mold can start to grow on dust caught deep in the pile and on the carpet back.

Day 3 to 7

The Carpet Breaks Down

As the latex bonding the backing gives out, the carpet delaminates and starts to ripple. The subfloor swells and the tack strip nails begin to rust.

Past one week

Past Saving

By now the carpet and pad usually have to come out. Mold has often reached the subfloor and the bottom of the drywall, so the job grows.

How We Fix It

Our Wet Carpet Drying Process

1

Map the Wet Area

We trace how far the water traveled with moisture meters and thermal imaging, including under walls and into closets. The water category gets identified as well, since it determines what we can keep.

2

Extract the Water

We use weighted extraction tools that squeeze water from carpet and pad at once. Drying goes faster afterward, because far more water comes out this way than with only a wand.

3

Dry Every Layer

Depending on the job, we float the carpet on moving air or lift it and remove the pad. Our air movers and dehumidifiers keep going until meters show a dry carpet and a dry subfloor.

4

Clean and Reset

We treat the carpet and subfloor with an antimicrobial where needed, lay new pad if the old one came out, restretch the carpet onto the tack strip, and clean it.
