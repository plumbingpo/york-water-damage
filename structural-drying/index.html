Of all the stages in restoring water damage, drying the structure counts most, and it is the stage most often botched by people without training. Extraction takes care of standing water, but drywall, framing, subflooring, insulation, concrete and other building materials stay loaded with trapped moisture. This trapped moisture must be scientifically removed through controlled evaporation and dehumidification to prevent the mold growth, wood rot, material degradation and odor problems that develop when structures remain wet.

York Restoration employs IICRC-certified structural drying technicians who understand psychrometry, the study of temperature, humidity and moisture relationships in air. For each job they apply it to calculate airflow patterns, temperature settings and the placement of equipment that will give the best conditions for evaporation. Because hardwood, drywall, plaster and concrete do not dry at the same speed, the method has to change with the material to bring it to acceptable moisture content without harming it further.

Every day of the drying process, we check moisture using thermal imaging cameras, thermo-hygrometers and calibrated moisture meters. Readings are recorded from start to finish in a detailed drying log, which shows moisture dropping step by step in every affected material. Equipment is adjusted daily based on these readings to optimize drying efficiency. We call a job dry when every affected material meets the moisture standard in the IICRC S500 guidelines, and never just because a surface feels dry.

Warning Signs for Structural Drying

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Gaps opening up in a hardwood floor, or planks with raised edges or centers
  • After water damage, a meter shows elevated moisture in the floor, wall or ceiling
  • Wallboard with water stains, puffiness or a spongy feel
  • Window glass that fogs up near the affected rooms
  • Swollen trim and baseboards separating at the seams
  • Wall finishes that start to peel or bubble in the days after water damage
  • Rooms that stay humid even with windows open and fans running

Structural Drying Prevention Tips

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Trust the moisture meter readings, because surfaces can feel dry while moisture remains deep in materials
  • The sooner drying starts after water gets in, the better things turn out, so do not wait
  • Plan for daily access so readings can be taken and equipment adjusted
  • Every hour of run time matters, so resist switching the machines off for some quiet
  • Dehumidifiers lose efficiency when outside air gets in, so keep drying areas closed up
  • Keep belongings off the machines and out of the path of the air movers

Why Hire a Professional for Structural Drying?

There is real science behind structural drying, which is why guessing does not work. Secondary mold damage most often traces back to someone who put down fans and hoped things would dry. It involves daily monitoring, a calculation of the required grain depression (the capacity to remove moisture) and a working knowledge of the way materials absorb moisture and let it go. Without this knowledge, under-drying leaves moisture that feeds mold, while over-drying can damage wood and other materials.

Consumer products and the equipment professionals dry structures with have very little in common. Commercial LGR (low-grain refrigerant) dehumidifiers remove 10 to 20 times more moisture from the air than residential dehumidifiers, and high-velocity air movers create the precise airflow patterns needed to accelerate evaporation from structural surfaces. This equipment represents a significant investment and requires training to deploy effectively, both reasons why professional drying produces far better outcomes than DIY attempts.
